Our Story

A Dream Brewed
Across Two Worlds

Sakura Akiyama spent four years running Temple Tokyo, one of Los Santos's most celebrated Japanese nightclubs. It was successful. It was celebrated. And eventually, it was not enough.

During her years at Temple Tokyo, Sakura made repeated trips to Japan, drawn by a need to understand not just what she was serving, but where it came from. Those trips led her to the breweries and distilleries of Fukushima, specifically Suehiro Sake Brewery, one of Japan's oldest and most revered producers, founded in 1850 in the heart of Aizu. She didn't visit as a tourist. She arrived as a student.

She sold everything. Closed Temple Tokyo. Moved to Fukushima. And spent years learning the full discipline of Japanese brewing from the ground up, working alongside Suehiro's master brewers through every season, every batch, every failure.

She returned to Los Santos a master. Blossom Breweries, founded in 2025, is everything those years were building toward. Not a bar. Not a restaurant. A craft.

Process

The Art of Patience

How we store, age, and finish every bottle.

Sakura Oak. Fukushima Barrels.

Blossom ages its liquors in Japanese Cherry Blossom oak, Sakura wood, barrels sourced directly from Fukushima. The same region where Sakura trained. The same land that shaped the craft.

Cherry Blossom oak is rare, prized, and quietly extraordinary. It imparts a delicate floral sweetness to everything it holds, and over time lends a subtle pink hue to the spirits resting within.

Time, Measured in Seasons.

Nothing leaves Blossom until it is ready. Sake rests for a minimum of three months. Whiskeys spend no less than two years in barrel. Specialty liquors, the rarest of our range, age as long as five years before a single bottle is filled.

Every batch is hand-checked by Sakura before approval. Unhurried by design. Blossom does not rush what time perfects.
